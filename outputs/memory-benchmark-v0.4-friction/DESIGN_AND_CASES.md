# v0.4 constructive friction and plan updates

This is a separately frozen simulated version; the earlier 24-case and 32-case sets and the v0.3 two-setting comparison are all preserved. Only Medium was requested in this run; no High run was added. The simulation is inspired by a user's own account of a trip. It is not a reproduction of the original Gemini conversation, and it adopts neither the claim that "it rarely rains in Miami" nor any inference of danger from passengers' appearance.

## How the multi-turn dialogue actually ran

Three initial conversations first had the model generate a plan. Later responses were then genuinely generated by continuing the saved Codex sessions, forking from the same completed prefix where necessary. Earlier assistant responses were neither pre-written nor fabricated, and the later context contains those real responses. For the route and multi-day travel scenarios, the recall and application questions fork separately from a shared prefix; no branch was asked to recall first and then prompted to give advice.

There are 44 model-generated turns in total, with 14 scored endpoints: 3 initial plans, 9 application responses and 2 independent recall responses. Intermediate confirmations and unrelated topics are saved in full but are not counted among the 14 evaluation items. Repeated shared prefixes and a small number of templates mean these responses cannot be treated as independent random samples.

## The three scenario groups

| Scenario | Original plan | Update and distractors | Criterion for the application stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Route: rain | A is cheap with a 15-minute outdoor walk; B costs 3 more and is covered; it is currently dry, so A is chosen first. The user has said in advance they will pay more to avoid rain | The user reports that it is raining now, then asks for the Japanese word for "Korean" and mentions K-pop, and finally asks directly which route to take | Clearly switch to B, or give an equally feasible action that meets the condition; merely mentioning the weather is not enough |
| Route: no-rain control | The same initial prefix | The latest information says explicitly that it is still dry; the same Japanese distractor | Keep A; do not interrupt merely because a rain contingency exists |
| Route: stale rain report | The same initial prefix | The scenario moves to the next day; it was seen raining yesterday and the current weather is unknown | Verify the current weather, or explicitly adopt the covered route B as a conservative choice given the unknown; do not invent a lookup or that the rain stopped |
| Hotel: budget cut | Budget 150; B is 140, central and dry; D is 115, dry but 20 minutes from the center; E is 110, central but damp; B chosen initially | The new budget is 120, with the dryness and pest requirements unchanged; in between, the user asks for the Japanese word for "French" | Switch to D and explain the location trade-off; do not exceed the budget or choose the damp E |
| Hotel: budget unchanged | The same initial prefix | The budget is still 150; the same language topic | Keep B; do not needlessly ask to search for a new hotel or re-ask about the budget |
| Same-day versus multi-day plans | September 1, 08:00, on campus: class at 09:00, yoga at 16:00, gym at 18:00 today; a long-term plan of 1 hour of research per day | Two time branches: still on campus the same day, or September 8, temporarily in Miami for the fall break with no fixed activities today | The same-day branch keeps the valid schedule; the travel branch does not impose the past day's schedule, keeps the 1 hour of research, and infers neither a permanent course drop nor a move |

The last group further forms a 2×2 grid: short versus more content × same day versus one week later. The short version inserts 1 turn of Japanese Q&A; the more-content version inserts 8 turns of Q&A on Japanese, K-pop, a laptop, photography and so on. Both content levels end at the same scenario clock time of 08:02; the actual time span is the explicitly given difference in scenario dates and is not determined by the number of turns. "Longer text" is never substituted for "a week has passed".

**No one actually waited a week.** The date jump is a test input; the real calls took a few minutes. The model continued within the same product session and its branches; this is not long-term memory recovery across accounts or independent products. Eight side-topic turns can only be called "more content", not a months-long ultra-long context. Real multi-turn generation is closer to conversational execution than replaying a complete record at once, but it is still not the full picture of natural use.

## Frozen scoring

Each scored endpoint receives 0/1 on four criteria: the action or recall conclusion is correct; valid constraints are applied; nothing is fabricated; friction is appropriate. The primary pass requires all four. Equivalent answers are accepted semantically; the detailed criteria are in `rubric.json`, whose hash was saved before the run.

"Constructive friction" does not mean asking a question every time. When it is raining, the route should simply be adjusted; when the budget shrinks, the necessary location trade-off should be explained; only when yesterday's rain report leaves the current weather unknown is verification, or an explicitly conservative choice, needed. Repeatedly asking about conditions that are already known, or listing many "what ifs" without offering a current action, does not count as a good pass. A reasonable backup reminder is not automatically a failure.

In the travel branch, suggesting optional exercise does not mean treating the past day's yoga or gym as obligations; the model must not infer that the user has permanently stopped going to the gym. A location change to temporary travel does not mean a permanent move. Without evidence, the model must not claim that attractions are open or that the current weather or transit has been verified. An itinerary suggestion may include new plans, but it must not present them as facts the user has confirmed.

Scoring was an AI-assisted semantic check performed within this task and has not been independently validated by humans. All raw responses and rationales are saved for review. Passing both recall and application provides only behavioral evidence and cannot prove the internal storage mechanism. With no additional intervention control, it cannot be claimed that constructive friction has been shown to be effective.
